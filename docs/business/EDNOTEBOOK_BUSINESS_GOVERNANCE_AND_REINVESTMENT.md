# EdNotebook Business Governance, Reinvestment, and Market Proof Doctrine

**Status:** Governing reference for product, revenue, fundraising, hiring, and platform investment decisions.

**Applies to:** EdNotebook and its Student, Professor, Institutional, K–12, Publishing, Research, Analytics, and Marketplace divisions.

**Owner:** Transform Ontology Systems, d/b/a McGaffie Industries LLC.

**Planning note:** Valuation and revenue figures in this document are internal planning ranges, not appraisals, guarantees, forecasts, or investment offers. They must be revised as actual revenue, retention, contracts, costs, and market evidence become available.

---

## 1. Governing market position

EdNotebook is not governed as a collection of unrelated features and should not be managed as a generic AI course builder.

EdNotebook is governed as:

> **The learning evidence, communication, and publishing infrastructure connecting students, professors, institutions, and educational content.**

The platform connects:

```text
Course intent
→ course materials
→ instruction
→ assignments and assessment
→ student activity
→ communication
→ evidence of learning
→ learning outcomes
→ intervention
→ later performance
→ institutional reporting
→ research
→ publishing and distribution
```

The commercial sequence is:

```text
Professor and teacher adoption is the sale.
Student return is the proof of daily usefulness.
Institutional trust is the path to durable contracts.
Improved outcomes are the evidence that makes the platform valuable.
```

Outcome and engagement data are the fuel, but data must be collected lawfully, transparently, securely, and only for defined educational, operational, or approved research purposes.

## 2. The four proof obligations

Every major product investment must support at least one of these obligations. Features that support none of them should be deferred, simplified, or removed from the active roadmap.

### 2.1 Professors and teachers adopt

The platform must reduce work that instructors already dislike doing.

**Absolutely necessary capabilities:**

- Syllabus upload, extraction, review, and calendar creation
- Course shell and lesson creation
- Assignments, quizzes, knowledge checks, and rubrics
- Learning-outcome creation and mapping
- Secure course materials and file handling
- Announcements that reach students
- Course and assignment messaging
- Office-hours scheduling
- Professor preview of the learner experience
- Grade and evidence review workflows
- Basic course analytics
- Reliable imports, exports, and integrations
- Fast onboarding and responsive support

**Adoption proof metrics:**

- Time to create the first course
- Time to upload and confirm a syllabus
- Percentage creating the first assignment
- Percentage publishing the first announcement
- Weekly professor return rate
- Courses still active after one term
- Reduction in repetitive student questions
- Instructor time saved
- Instructor satisfaction and recommendation

### 2.2 Students return

Students must receive immediate, repeated value without being forced to learn another complicated application.

**Absolutely necessary capabilities:**

- Public syllabus drop before account creation
- Due-Next and calendar
- Assignment reminders
- Course rooms and professor communication
- Mobile-responsive experience
- Push notifications when the native application is activated
- Offline calendar access
- Shared assignment spaces where allowed
- Secure materials and interactive reading
- Discover and Discover Notebook foundations
- Clear privacy and data choices
- Easy account creation after receiving value
- Section-level network effects and class-forming notifications

**Return proof metrics:**

- Syllabus uploads completed
- Guest-to-account conversion
- Day-7 and Day-30 student retention
- Weekly active learners
- Due-Next opens
- Assignment completion
- Message and announcement engagement
- Shared-space use
- Course-section density
- Referral conversion

### 2.3 Institutions trust

Institutional trust is earned through reliability, security, governance, evidence, accessibility, and interoperability—not through feature volume.

**Absolutely necessary capabilities:**

- Multi-tenant organizations and role-based access
- Strong Row Level Security and negative-access tests
- Quarantine-first file uploads
- Malware and archive inspection
- Audited short-lived downloads
- Retention and legal holds
- Consent, privacy, FERPA-supporting controls, and data inventory
- Accessibility documentation and testing
- Incident response and audit export
- Secure communications and moderation administration
- SSO and standards-based integrations when activated
- LTI 1.3, OneRoster, QTI, Common Cartridge, and Open Badges roadmap
- Reliable reporting and data export
- Transparent AI governance
- Institutional pilot and support playbooks

**Trust proof metrics:**

- Security review findings closed
- Accessibility defects closed
- Uptime and incident record
- Pilot renewal
- Department expansion
- Institutional conversion rate
- Implementation time
- Support response and resolution time
- Data export and deletion requests completed correctly
- No cross-course or cross-institution data leakage

### 2.4 Learning outcomes improve

EdNotebook must demonstrate that it does more than increase clicks or time on platform.

**Absolutely necessary capabilities:**

- Learning-outcome builder
- Outcome-to-lesson, assignment, question, and rubric mapping
- Evidence-item capture
- Mastery history
- Pre/post and repeated-measure comparisons
- Intervention records
- Course feedback and surveys
- Quantitative and qualitative analysis
- Evidence Studio reporting
- Data-quality and limitation sections
- Research-governance controls
- De-identified and restricted export options

**Outcome proof metrics:**

- Mastery improvement
- Assessment improvement
- Course completion
- Retention in course or program
- Intervention response
- Reduction in missing assignments
- Student confidence and satisfaction
- Instructor-reported usefulness
- Replication across courses or cohorts

No causal claim should be made from correlation alone. Outcome claims require a documented method, limitations, data quality, and appropriate approval.

## 3. Replacement cost does not mean a one-time rebuild

The earlier **$2 million–$8 million replacement-cost estimate** describes what a professionally staffed organization might spend to recreate the planned breadth, security, integrations, mobile applications, analytics, publishing, institutional readiness, testing, and operations from the beginning.

It does **not** mean EdNotebook should stop and spend $2 million–$8 million on a single rebuild.

The preferred model is a **continuous staged rebuild**:

```text
Founder-built product
→ user proof
→ revenue
→ reinvestment
→ professional hardening
→ larger customers
→ more revenue
→ deeper reinvestment
```

The codebase should be upgraded module by module while preserving working systems. The company purchases professional quality progressively instead of buying the full mature-company architecture before product-market evidence exists.

### Governing rule

> **EdNotebook will continuously reinvest a defined share of gross profit and financing proceeds into the professional rebuilding, hardening, testing, documentation, accessibility, security, integrations, data systems, and operating capacity required for its next revenue stage.**

This is not an endless rewrite. Each reinvestment must address an identified bottleneck tied to adoption, return, trust, or outcomes.

## 4. When professional rebuilding becomes necessary

There is no single valuation point at which the entire product must be rebuilt. A $1 million pre-seed valuation does not itself create a technical rebuilding requirement.

The obligation begins when any of these conditions becomes true:

1. Real students, instructors, or institutions depend on the system.
2. A failure could expose protected educational records or materially disrupt coursework.
3. Paid customers expect reliability, support, accessibility, or contractual commitments.
4. The founder becomes the only person capable of maintaining critical production systems.
5. Manual processes prevent new revenue or make support costs unsustainable.
6. Institutional security review identifies required remediation.
7. Usage, storage, Realtime traffic, AI cost, or reporting volume exceeds the current architecture.
8. A feature becomes material to revenue but lacks tests, observability, documentation, or ownership.
9. Native mobile distribution, publisher payouts, or external integrations introduce new platform obligations.
10. The company is preparing to accept outside capital based on claims of scalable technology.

### Absolute minimum rebuilding point

Professional hardening should begin **before** the first material institutional deployment, not after it fails.

A practical trigger is the earliest of:

- 3–5 active professors using real courses
- 100–500 active students
- First paid institutional pilot
- First $100,000 of annualized recurring revenue
- First contract containing security, uptime, support, data-protection, or accessibility commitments

At that point, revenue or financing should fund at least part-time professional engineering, QA/security review, documentation, and production monitoring.

### When a dedicated team becomes necessary

A small dedicated product and engineering team becomes difficult to avoid at the earliest of:

- $250,000–$500,000 ARR
- 1,000–5,000 monthly active learners
- 10–20 active institutional or department customers
- Multiple live integrations
- Material publishing transactions
- Mobile application launch
- Formal uptime or support commitments

### When a mature rebuild program becomes necessary

At approximately $1 million ARR or when serving several institutions, the company should no longer treat professional rebuilding as occasional project work. It should become an annual operating program with a staffed roadmap, budget, service ownership, security review, and release management.

The company should not wait for $1 million ARR to fix critical security, privacy, accessibility, or data-isolation defects. Critical risks are repaired immediately, even before meaningful revenue.

## 5. Reinvestment doctrine

### 5.1 Definitions

**Revenue:** Money received from subscriptions, contracts, services, marketplace commissions, publishing, implementation, training, analytics, and other lawful operations.

**Gross profit:** Revenue remaining after direct costs required to deliver that revenue, including payment processing, model usage, storage, document processing, marketplace payouts, and directly attributable support or hosting costs.

**Operating profit:** Gross profit after payroll, contractors, marketing, legal, insurance, administration, and other operating expenses.

Investment decisions must not confuse revenue with available cash.

### 5.2 Default allocation rule

Until EdNotebook reaches durable product-market fit and maintains an adequate cash reserve, the target allocation of **gross profit** is:

| Use | Target share of gross profit |
|---|---:|
| Product rebuilding, engineering, QA, security, accessibility, and infrastructure | **35%–50%** |
| Customer implementation, support, and success | 15%–25% |
| Sales, pilots, partnerships, and responsible growth | 15%–25% |
| Legal, compliance, insurance, finance, and administration | 5%–15% |
| Cash reserve and contingency | 10%–20% |

Percentages may overlap during small-revenue stages because one contractor or employee can support more than one category. The governing principle is that **the rebuild share should not be treated as optional founder compensation or general surplus.**

### 5.3 Minimum reinvestment floor

Before stable profitability, EdNotebook should ordinarily reinvest at least:

- **40% of gross profit** while annual revenue is below $500,000
- **30% of gross profit** from $500,000 to $2 million annual revenue
- **20%–25% of gross profit** above $2 million annual revenue, unless the product is already meeting mature reliability, security, accessibility, and integration standards

Outside investment should generally allocate:

- 45%–60% to product, engineering, security, data, and infrastructure
- 15%–25% to pilots, implementation, and customer success
- 10%–20% to distribution, partnerships, and sales
- 5%–15% to legal, finance, insurance, and contingency

These are planning targets. Cash runway and contractual obligations take priority.

### 5.4 Reinvestment decision test

Every rebuilding expense should answer:

1. Which proof obligation does this support?
2. Which measurable bottleneck does it remove?
3. What revenue or risk depends on it?
4. Can the current system be hardened rather than replaced?
5. What is the smallest professional increment that solves the problem?
6. How will success be measured?
7. What operating cost will it add?
8. Who owns it after launch?

### Stage 0 — Pre-revenue to $100,000 annual revenue

**Primary objective:** Validate professor adoption and student return without creating institutional risk.

**Rebuild priorities:**

- Stabilize production domain and deployment
- Authentication and role correctness
- Course, assignment, calendar, and syllabus reliability
- Secure upload and download pipeline
- Data backups and migration discipline
- Error monitoring and audit logging
- Basic accessibility
- Automated build and smoke tests
- Privacy, terms, and data-use documentation
- Pilot support procedures

**Recommended team:**

- Founder/product lead
- Part-time senior full-stack engineer or technical advisor
- Contract QA/security review at milestones
- Instructional-design and professor beta advisors

**Investment guide:** $25,000–$100,000 accumulated professional hardening, funded through founder capital, services, grants, pilot revenue, or pre-seed funds.

### Stage 1 — $100,000 to $500,000 ARR

**Primary objective:** Convert professor-led pilots into department and institutional contracts.

**Rebuild priorities:**

- Production observability and incident response
- Automated RLS and integration testing
- Messaging and moderation control plane
- Institutional tenancy and delegated administration
- Accessibility audit and remediation
- Reliable reporting and exports
- Support-case system
- SSO/LTI foundations
- Cost monitoring for AI, storage, and processing
- Documentation and onboarding

**Recommended team:**

- 1 senior full-stack/platform engineer
- 1 product/frontend engineer or strong contractor
- Fractional security/privacy support
- Customer success/implementation support

**Annual product/rebuild budget:** approximately $150,000–$400,000, adjusted to financing and margin.

### Stage 2 — $500,000 to $2 million ARR

**Primary objective:** Establish repeatable institutional delivery and measurable outcome proof.

**Rebuild priorities:**

- Dedicated platform/API architecture
- Institutional integrations
- Mobile/Capacitor hardening and native operations
- Data warehouse or governed analytics layer
- Evidence Studio and outcome reporting
- Disaster recovery
- Formal vulnerability management
- Performance and load testing
- Publisher rights, entitlements, and transactions
- Privacy-safe aggregated analytics
- Dedicated support and release management

**Recommended team:**

- Engineering lead
- 2–4 software engineers
- QA/automation engineer
- Data/analytics engineer
- Product designer
- Security/privacy support
- Customer success and implementation

**Annual product/rebuild budget:** approximately $400,000–$1.2 million.

### Stage 3 — $2 million to $10 million ARR

**Primary objective:** Scale across institutions, publishing, and selected K–12 customers.

**Rebuild priorities:**

- Service ownership and platform reliability
- Dedicated data governance
- Integration marketplace
- Advanced interoperability
- Publisher reporting and payout operations
- Regional and institutional configuration
- Mature mobile release process
- Advanced accessibility governance
- Penetration testing and compliance programs
- Scalable customer implementation

**Recommended team:** 8–20 staff across engineering, product, data, security, implementation, and support.

**Annual product/rebuild budget:** approximately $1 million–$4 million.

### Stage 4 — Above $10 million ARR

**Primary objective:** Operate EdNotebook as a multi-sided education platform.

The company should now maintain a formal annual platform modernization budget instead of thinking in terms of a one-time rebuild. Modules are continually replaced, consolidated, or hardened according to usage, risk, customer commitments, and strategic advantage.

## 7. How staged reinvestment reduces total rebuilding cost

A staged rebuild can cost less than a speculative $2 million–$8 million reconstruction because EdNotebook does not need to professionally rebuild every planned feature before knowing whether customers use it.

The process reduces waste by:

- Rebuilding only validated workflows
- Deleting or deferring low-use concepts before professional investment
- Using revenue to reveal the actual bottleneck
- Reusing shared infrastructure across divisions
- Avoiding separate systems for messaging, entitlements, analytics, files, and identity
- Preserving additive migrations and compatible interfaces
- Hiring specialists only when their domain becomes material
- Negotiating vendor costs after usage is known
- Funding infrastructure from contracts that require it

However, delaying critical controls can increase total cost. These areas are never deferred merely to save money:

- Authentication and authorization
- Cross-tenant isolation
- Security defects
- Privacy and consent
- Data loss prevention
- Accessibility blockers
- Payment verification
- Rights and piracy controls
- Retention and legal holds
- Reliable backups and recovery

## 10. Valuation governance

### Current planning position

A pre-seed planning valuation around **$1 million** can be used as an internal or negotiation starting point, but it is not established merely because the code would be expensive to reproduce.

Valuation strengthens as EdNotebook proves:

1. Professors adopt.
2. Students return.
3. Institutions trust the system.
4. Outcomes improve.
5. Revenue is recurring.
6. Gross margins are understandable.
7. The founder is not the sole operational dependency.

### Valuation milestones

| Evidence stage | Internal planning range |
|---|---:|
| Product and architecture, limited sustained use | $500K–$2M |
| Stable beta and credible active cohort | $1M–$5M |
| Signed institutional pilot and outcome plan | $3M–$8M |
| $250K–$500K ARR with retention | $5M–$12M |
| Approximately $1M ARR with strong growth | $10M–$25M |
| $5M ARR with durable institutional retention | $30M–$75M |

These are scenario ranges, not independent valuations.

### Use of replacement cost

Replacement cost should support the narrative that EdNotebook has accumulated substantial technical and architectural assets. It should not be presented as proof that the company is worth the full replacement cost.

The better investor statement is:

> EdNotebook has built the foundation of a platform that would require substantial professional cost to reproduce. The company’s governing strategy is to convert professor-led adoption into revenue and continuously reinvest that revenue into professional hardening, reducing speculative rebuild waste while increasing institutional readiness at each stage.

## 13. Governance gates

A new feature enters active development only when it satisfies at least one of these tests:

- Removes a professor adoption barrier
- Increases student return or course density
- Closes an institutional trust requirement
- Produces stronger outcome evidence
- Directly enables contracted or validated revenue
- Reduces material operating or security risk
- Strengthens reusable IP without weakening the core roadmap

A feature is paused when:

- It duplicates an existing system
- It creates a second identity, messaging, entitlement, analytics, or storage architecture
- It has no owner after launch
- It increases compliance exposure without corresponding value
- It cannot be measured
- It consumes rebuilding capital needed by P0 systems

## 14. Quarterly board/founder review

Review this doctrine quarterly using actual data.

Required questions:

1. Which professors adopted and returned?
2. Which students returned after seven and thirty days?
3. Which institutions advanced, renewed, expanded, or declined?
4. What evidence of learning improvement exists?
5. What revenue was earned by division?
6. What gross profit was available?
7. What percentage was reinvested into rebuilding?
8. Which technical risks could block the next customer?
9. Which features were unused and should be deferred?
10. Which IP assets became more reusable or defensible?
11. Is the founder still a single point of failure?
12. What professional capability must be purchased next?

Publish an internal quarterly record in:

```text
docs/business/quarterly/
```

The record should include revenue, gross margin, reinvestment, product reliability, adoption, retention, institutional pipeline, outcome evidence, and unresolved risks.

## 15. Final governing rule

> **EdNotebook will not wait for a multimillion-dollar one-time rebuild. It will earn the rebuild through professor-led adoption, student return, institutional trust, and demonstrated learning outcomes. Revenue and financing will be reinvested continuously into professional quality, beginning before the first material institutional deployment and increasing with the platform’s obligations.**

The company’s objective is not to preserve every early implementation. It is to preserve customer data, working capabilities, product knowledge, and validated workflows while progressively replacing fragile implementation with maintainable professional systems.
