# EdNotebook Business Governance, Growth, and Reinvestment Doctrine

**Status:** Governing reference for product focus, partnerships, revenue, fundraising, hiring, and platform investment decisions.

**Applies to:** EdNotebook and its Student, Professor, Institutional, K–12, Publishing, Research, Analytics, and Marketplace divisions.

**Owner:** Transform Ontology Systems, d/b/a McGaffie Industries LLC.

**Planning notice:** Valuation and revenue figures in this document are internal planning ranges, not appraisals, guarantees, forecasts, or investment offers. They must be revised as actual revenue, retention, contracts, costs, and market evidence become available.

---

## 1. Governing market position

EdNotebook is not governed as a collection of unrelated features and should not be managed as a generic AI course builder.

EdNotebook is governed as:

> **The learning evidence, communication, and publishing infrastructure connecting students, professors, institutions, and educational content.**

The platform connects:

```text
Course intent
→ course materials
→ instruction
→ assignments and assessment
→ student activity
→ communication
→ evidence of learning
→ learning outcomes
→ intervention
→ later performance
→ institutional reporting
→ research
→ publishing and distribution
```

The commercial sequence is:

```text
Professor and teacher adoption is the sale.
Student return is the proof of daily usefulness.
Institutional trust is the path to durable contracts.
Improved outcomes are the evidence that makes the platform valuable.
```

Outcome and engagement data are the fuel, but data must be collected lawfully, transparently, securely, and only for defined educational, operational, or approved research purposes.

## 2. Current operating stage

EdNotebook has grown beyond a simple prompt or isolated demonstration. The platform shell and many major capabilities now exist.

That progress creates a new governing priority:

> **The next stage is not feature expansion. The next stage is to finish, connect, test, simplify, and reveal the platform in controlled stages.**

The work now is to:

1. Finish the core professor and student journeys.
2. Test account, course, syllabus, assignment, communication, file, and reporting flows end to end.
3. Connect features that currently operate separately.
4. Split oversized folders and modules so the platform remains stable and maintainable.
5. Reduce crashes, blank states, and fragile loading behavior.
6. Hide or mute unfinished features from ordinary users.
7. Preserve later-stage features behind controlled activation.
8. Reveal capabilities only when they are useful, tested, supportable, and connected to the four proof areas.

### Product-focus rule

> **No new major feature category should be added until the professor-adoption, student-return, institutional-trust, and learning-outcome journeys are stable enough for controlled real-user testing.**

Existing unfinished features should not be destroyed. They should be preserved, documented, tested separately, and activated later through feature controls.

## 3. The four proof obligations

Every major product investment must support at least one of these obligations. Features that support none of them should be deferred, simplified, or removed from the active first-release experience.

### 3.1 Professors and teachers adopt

The platform must reduce work that instructors already dislike doing while helping them teach and communicate more effectively.

**Absolutely necessary experiences:**

- Upload a syllabus and turn dates into a usable course schedule.
- Create a course without starting from an empty screen.
- Add lessons, assignments, quizzes, knowledge checks, and rubrics.
- Define learning outcomes.
- Organize course files and links in the correct place.
- Preview the course as a student.
- Send announcements students notice.
- Answer course and assignment questions in one place.
- Offer office-hour appointment slots.
- Review student progress and submitted work.
- View useful class summaries.
- Export records and reports.

**Adoption proof metrics:**

- Time to create the first course
- Time to upload and confirm a syllabus
- First assignment created
- First announcement sent
- First student invited
- Weekly professor return
- Instructor time saved
- Reduction in repetitive student questions
- Courses active after one term
- Instructor recommendation

### 3.2 Students return

Students must receive immediate and repeated value without being forced to learn another complicated application.

**Absolutely necessary experiences:**

- Upload a syllabus before account creation.
- See what is due next.
- Keep a course calendar and reminders.
- Receive professor announcements.
- Ask questions in course or assignment spaces.
- Open course materials without searching multiple systems.
- Read interactive course books and take notes.
- Save and compare research papers.
- Work with classmates when permitted.
- Use the platform effectively on a phone.
- Keep a clear record of course progress.
- Understand and control optional data uses.

**Return proof metrics:**

- Syllabus uploads completed
- Guest-to-account conversion
- Day-7 and Day-30 student return
- Weekly active learners
- Due-Next opens
- Assignment completion
- Announcement opens
- Course-message use
- Shared-space use
- Course-section density
- Referral conversion

### 3.3 Institutions trust

Institutional trust is earned through reliability, security, governance, evidence, accessibility, and compatibility—not through feature volume.

**Absolutely necessary assurances:**

- Clear organization and role controls
- Separation between institutions, courses, and users
- Safe file upload and delivery
- Malware and archive inspection
- Communication rules and moderation controls
- Audit records
- Retention and legal-hold rules
- Privacy and consent choices
- Accessibility testing
- Incident and support procedures
- Reliable data exports
- Clear AI boundaries
- Standards-based institutional connections
- Pilot and implementation plans

**Trust proof metrics:**

- Security findings closed
- Accessibility findings closed
- No cross-course data exposure
- No cross-institution data exposure
- Support response and resolution time
- Pilot renewal
- Department expansion
- Institutional conversion
- Data export and deletion completed correctly
- Uptime and incident record

### 3.4 Learning outcomes improve

EdNotebook must demonstrate that it does more than increase clicks or time on the platform.

**Absolutely necessary evidence experiences:**

- Define what students should learn.
- Connect lessons, assignments, questions, and rubrics to learning outcomes.
- Collect evidence from student work.
- Track improvement over time.
- Record support or intervention.
- Compare performance before and after instruction.
- Collect student and instructor feedback.
- Combine numerical results with written feedback.
- Generate reports that explain methods, limitations, and findings.
- Export approved data for evaluation or research.

**Outcome proof metrics:**

- Outcome mastery improvement
- Assessment improvement
- Course completion
- Retention in course or program
- Missing-assignment reduction
- Response to interventions
- Student confidence
- Instructor-reported usefulness
- Replication across courses and terms

No causal claim should be made from correlation alone.

## 4. Digital Literacy Course as product proof

The Digital Literacy Course is a working example of the EdNotebook direction.

It demonstrates that educational content can be organized into:

- A clear starting point
- Structured chapters
- Guided activities
- Quizzes and checks
- A learner journey
- Completion progress
- Visible achievement
- Accessible web delivery

It should be used as proof of how EdNotebook can:

1. Turn a curriculum into an interactive course.
2. Give professors a reusable course structure.
3. Give students a guided learning experience.
4. Collect course and outcome evidence.
5. Publish educational material through the platform.
6. Repeat the model for Financial Literacy, Research Literacy, institutional training, publisher supplements, and other subjects.

The intended pathway is:

```text
Digital Literacy Course
→ tested learning design
→ EdNotebook course template
→ professor customization
→ student use
→ evidence and feedback
→ improved future editions
```

## 5. Feature activation doctrine

The complete feature set is governed by:

```text
docs/business/EDNOTEBOOK_FEATURE_ACTIVATION_ROADMAP.md
```

Every feature must be categorized as:

- Ready to demonstrate now
- Built in part and being connected
- Preserved but hidden until later
- Planned after proof and partnerships

A feature may become visible only when:

1. Its user journey is complete.
2. Its data is stored correctly.
3. Its access rules are tested.
4. Error and recovery states exist.
5. Mobile behavior is acceptable.
6. Accessibility has been reviewed.
7. Security and privacy effects are understood.
8. Support documentation exists.
9. It supports at least one of the four proof obligations.
10. A named person owns it after launch.

## 9. Replacement cost and staged investment

The earlier $2 million–$8 million replacement-cost estimate describes what a professional team might spend to recreate the planned breadth, security, integrations, mobile applications, analytics, publishing, institutional readiness, testing, and operations from the beginning.

It does not mean EdNotebook should pause for a one-time rewrite.

The preferred model is:

```text
Working platform
→ user proof
→ revenue
→ focused reinvestment
→ professional improvement
→ larger customers
→ deeper reinvestment
```

### Governing financial rule

> **EdNotebook will continuously reinvest a defined share of gross profit and financing into reliability, testing, security, accessibility, integrations, data systems, customer support, and the people required for its next revenue stage.**

This financial discipline belongs inside the finance section of the business presentation. It is not the presentation’s main story.

### Suggested gross-profit allocation before durable product-market fit

| Use | Planning range |
|---|---:|
| Product quality, engineering, testing, security, accessibility, and infrastructure | 35%–50% |
| Customer implementation and support | 15%–25% |
| Pilots, partnerships, distribution, and sales | 15%–25% |
| Legal, compliance, insurance, and administration | 5%–15% |
| Reserve and contingency | 10%–20% |

Revenue is not the same as available cash. Direct delivery costs must be paid before gross profit is calculated.

### Staged investment

#### Before $100K annual revenue

Focus on:

- Core reliability
- Professor and student pilot readiness
- Security and privacy fundamentals
- Support and monitoring

Planning investment:

- $25K–$100K accumulated professional hardening

#### $100K–$500K ARR

Focus on:

- Professional support
- Automated testing
- Institution readiness
- Accessibility
- Messaging and moderation
- Reliable reports

Planning annual investment:

- $150K–$400K

#### $500K–$2M ARR

Focus on:

- Small dedicated team
- Stronger data and outcome systems
- Mobile hardening
- Integrations
- Publishing transactions

Planning annual investment:

- $400K–$1.2M

#### $2M–$10M ARR

Focus on:

- Multiple institutions
- Publishing growth
- Selected K–12 entry
- Integration ecosystem
- Formal operating programs

Planning annual investment:

- $1M–$4M

A $1 million valuation does not itself trigger a rebuild. Real users, contracts, privacy obligations, security risks, support demands, and scale trigger professional investment.

## 10. Purpose, vision, goals, and timeline

## Purpose

Help educators turn course plans and educational materials into courses students can follow while producing useful evidence of learning.

## Vision

Create a platform where teaching, student work, communication, publishing, and outcome evidence reinforce each other instead of living in disconnected systems.

## Near-term goal

Finish and test the core higher-education experience with professors and students.

## Medium-term goal

Convert successful professor-led use into department and institutional pilots.

## Long-term goal

Become a trusted institutional learning, evidence, and publishing platform, beginning with higher education and expanding carefully into publishing and K–12.

### Current through controlled beta

- Stabilize the platform shell
- Split oversized folders and modules
- Finish core professor and student paths
- Hide unfinished features
- Test with a small number of professors and students
- Collect structured feedback

### First successful term

- Improve based on real use
- Produce the first professor case study
- Produce student return evidence
- Complete institution-facing documentation
- Begin paid department pilots

### Following 12–24 months

- Expand professor-led adoption
- Add institutional connections
- Strengthen outcome reporting
- Activate selected publishing capabilities
- Prepare native mobile distribution

### Following 24–60 months

- Expand institutional contracts
- Grow Alex B. Morrison Library
- Introduce privacy-protected publisher intelligence
- Enter selected K–12 pilots after readiness
- Build a multi-division education platform
