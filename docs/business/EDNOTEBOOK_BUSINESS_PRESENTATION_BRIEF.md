# EdNotebook Business Presentation Brief

**Purpose:** Governing source for the public EdNotebook business presentation.

**Audience:** Potential business partners, strategic collaborators, professors, teachers, university leaders, publishers, authors, funders, advisors, future team members, and institutions considering a pilot.

**Primary public location:** A dedicated Business Presentation page linked from both:

1. The Work With Us area.
2. The bottom of the main EdNotebook landing page.

**Owner:** Transform Ontology Systems, d/b/a McGaffie Industries LLC.

**Planning notice:** Revenue, valuation, adoption, and growth figures are internal planning scenarios. They are not guarantees, appraisals, investment offers, or claims of current performance.

---

## 1. Presentation purpose

The presentation must explain EdNotebook as a growing education platform and invite the viewer to understand where it is going, what has already been built, what must happen next, and where a partner could contribute.

It must not be presented as a software-rebuild presentation.

The main story is:

```text
Professors and teachers adopt
→ students return
→ institutions trust
→ learning outcomes improve
```

The presentation should make clear that:

- Professor and teacher adoption is the first sale.
- Student return proves that the platform has lasting daily value.
- Institutional adoption is the long-term commercial goal.
- Learning evidence and outcome data are the fuel that proves EdNotebook is useful.

The presentation should feel like a founder showing a respected colleague:

- What has been built
- What problem it solves
- Why it matters
- How the parts reinforce each other
- What is ready now
- What should be activated later
- How revenue can grow
- What kind of help would accelerate the project

Do not use language such as “this is a cool project” or “be my business partner” directly. The design and narrative should naturally communicate opportunity, seriousness, momentum, and an invitation to participate.

## 3. Current operating stage

EdNotebook has grown beyond a simple demonstration or a single AI prompt.

The platform shell and many major feature areas exist. That is positive evidence that the product is substantial, but it also changes the next objective.

The current objective is no longer to add more feature categories.

The current objective is:

1. Finish the core user journeys.
2. Test the working features end to end.
3. Connect features that currently operate separately.
4. Split oversized folders or modules so the application remains stable and maintainable.
5. Reduce crashes and blank states.
6. Simplify what is visible to the first users.
7. Hide or mute later-stage capabilities until their foundations are tested.
8. Reveal capabilities in stages as the four proof areas are demonstrated.

### Governing product rule

> **No new major feature category should be added until the core professor, student, institutional-trust, and learning-outcome journeys are stable enough to test with real users.**

Existing future-facing features should not be deleted. They should be:

- Preserved
- Documented
- Feature-flagged
- Hidden from ordinary users when unfinished
- Tested separately
- Activated deliberately

This protects the work already completed without overwhelming users or increasing instability.

## 4. The four business proof areas

## 4.1 Professors and teachers adopt

This is the first sale and the initial distribution engine.

The professor or teacher must quickly understand that EdNotebook can reduce work, organize the course, improve communication, and help show what students learned.

### Necessary professor-facing experiences

- Upload a syllabus and turn dates into a usable course schedule.
- Create a course without starting from an empty screen.
- Add lessons, assignments, quizzes, knowledge checks, and rubrics.
- Organize course files and links in the correct lesson or assignment.
- See the course as a student will see it.
- Send announcements students are likely to notice.
- Answer course and assignment questions in one place.
- Offer office-hour appointment slots.
- Review student progress and submitted work.
- Define learning outcomes and connect work to those outcomes.
- View useful class summaries without becoming a data analyst.
- Export records and reports when needed.

### Professor adoption proof

Measure:

- Time to create the first course
- Time to upload and approve a syllabus
- First assignment created
- First announcement sent
- First student invited
- Weekly professor return
- Instructor time saved
- Reduction in repetitive questions
- Course continuation after one term
- Instructor recommendation

---

## 4.2 Students return

Students must receive immediate value and then have a reason to return every week.

### Necessary student-facing experiences

- Upload a syllabus before creating an account.
- See what is due next.
- Keep a course calendar and reminders.
- Receive professor announcements.
- Ask questions in course or assignment spaces.
- Open course materials without hunting through unrelated pages.
- Read interactive course books and take notes.
- Save research papers and compare them.
- Work with classmates when collaboration is allowed.
- Use the platform effectively on a phone.
- Keep a clear record of course progress.
- Understand what data is collected and control optional uses.

### Student return proof

Measure:

- Syllabus uploads
- Guest-to-account conversion
- Day-7 and Day-30 return
- Weekly active students
- Due-Next opens
- Assignment completion
- Announcement opens
- Course-message use
- Shared-space use
- Research notebook use
- Section growth and referrals

---

## 4.3 Institutions trust

Institutional adoption is the long-term commercial goal.

An institution will not purchase EdNotebook only because it has many features. It must trust the platform to handle users, records, files, communication, reporting, and support responsibly.

### Necessary institution-facing assurances

- Clear organization and role controls
- Separation between institutions, courses, and users
- Safe file upload and delivery
- Malware and archive inspection
- Communication rules and moderation controls
- Audit records
- Retention and legal-hold rules
- Privacy and consent choices
- Accessibility testing
- Incident and support procedures
- Reliable data export
- Clear AI boundaries
- Standards-based connections to institutional systems
- Pilot and implementation plans

### Institutional trust proof

Measure:

- Security findings closed
- Accessibility findings closed
- No cross-course data exposure
- No cross-institution data exposure
- Support response and resolution time
- Pilot renewal
- Department expansion
- Institutional conversion
- Data export and deletion completed correctly
- Uptime and incident record

---

## 4.4 Learning outcomes improve

This is the evidence that makes EdNotebook more valuable than a scheduling or content-delivery tool.

### Necessary learning-evidence experiences

- Define what students should learn.
- Connect lessons, assignments, questions, and rubrics to learning outcomes.
- Collect evidence from student work.
- Track improvement over time.
- Record support or intervention.
- Compare performance before and after instruction.
- Collect student and instructor feedback.
- Combine numerical results with written feedback.
- Generate reports that explain methods, limitations, and findings.
- Export approved data for program evaluation or research.

### Outcome proof

Measure:

- Outcome mastery improvement
- Assessment improvement
- Course completion
- Retention in course or program
- Missing-assignment reduction
- Response to interventions
- Student confidence
- Instructor-reported usefulness
- Replication across courses and terms

Do not present correlation as proof that EdNotebook caused an outcome.

## 5. Digital Literacy Course as proof

The Digital Literacy Course should be presented as a concrete example of the EdNotebook model.

It demonstrates that educational content can be organized into:

- A clear starting point
- Structured chapters
- Guided learning activities
- Quizzes and checks
- A learner journey
- Completion progress
- A certificate or visible achievement
- Accessible web delivery
- A public example that can be tested before institutional adoption

The course is not presented as a separate unrelated website.

It becomes proof of how EdNotebook can:

1. Turn a curriculum into an interactive course.
2. Give professors a reusable course structure.
3. Give students a guided experience.
4. Collect course and outcome evidence.
5. Publish educational material through the platform.
6. Demonstrate a model that can be repeated for Financial Literacy, Research Literacy, institutional training, publisher supplements, and other subjects.

The presentation should link to the live Digital Literacy Course when the link is confirmed in the repository.

It should show a pathway such as:

```text
Digital Literacy Course
→ tested learning design
→ EdNotebook course template
→ professor customization
→ student use
→ evidence and feedback
→ improved future editions
```

## 6. Product focus: finish, connect, simplify, reveal

The current platform contains more capability than the first public version should expose.

The launch strategy is:

```text
Preserve the full platform
→ stabilize the core
→ hide unfinished areas
→ test with professors and students
→ reveal the next feature only when it supports proven demand
```

### What must happen now

- Complete the professor course journey.
- Complete the student course journey.
- Test login, course creation, syllabus use, assignments, announcements, messaging, and reports.
- Connect the major features to the same account, course, and role system.
- Split oversized code folders and modules into stable sections.
- Improve loading, error, empty, and recovery states.
- Add feature flags for later-stage areas.
- Create a simple first-use experience.
- Run a controlled professor and student pilot.
- Collect structured feedback.

### What should not happen now

- Adding another major platform division
- Exposing every unfinished screen
- Launching K–12 broadly before higher-education proof
- Launching a marketplace before rights and payments are ready
- Presenting planned analytics as existing evidence
- Expanding AI features before the core course workflow is dependable

## 10. Financial discipline and staged investment

This section belongs inside the financial story, not at the center of the presentation.

Explain that the professional replacement-cost range of $2 million–$8 million is not a bill due today and is not the valuation.

EdNotebook should be improved in stages as users, revenue, contracts, and responsibilities grow.

### Financial governing rule

> A defined share of gross profit and financing will be reinvested into product reliability, testing, security, accessibility, integrations, support, and the people required for the next customer stage.

Suggested early allocation of gross profit:

| Use | Planning range |
|---|---:|
| Product quality, engineering, testing, security, accessibility, and infrastructure | 35%–50% |
| Customer implementation and support | 15%–25% |
| Pilots, partnerships, distribution, and sales | 15%–25% |
| Legal, compliance, insurance, and administration | 5%–15% |
| Reserve and contingency | 10%–20% |

The presentation should show staged investment briefly:

- Before $100K annual revenue: core reliability and pilot readiness
- $100K–$500K ARR: professional support, testing, institution readiness
- $500K–$2M ARR: small dedicated team and stronger data systems
- $2M–$10M ARR: multiple institutions, publishing, integrations, selected K–12
- Above $10M ARR: mature multi-division operations

Do not make this the opening or dominant narrative.

## 12. Purpose, vision, goals, and timeline

Help educators turn course plans and educational materials into courses students can follow, while producing useful evidence of learning.

## Vision

Create a platform where teaching, student work, communication, publishing, and outcome evidence reinforce each other instead of living in disconnected systems.

## Near-term goal

Finish and test the core higher-education experience with professors and students.

## Medium-term goal

Convert successful professor-led use into department and institutional pilots.

Become a trusted institutional learning, evidence, and publishing platform with higher education as the initial market and carefully staged expansion into publishing and K–12.

### Now through controlled beta

- Stabilize the platform shell
- Split oversized modules
- Finish the core professor and student paths

- Expand professor-led adoption
- Add institutional connections

- Prepare native mobile distribution

### Following 24–60 months

- Expand institutional contracts
- Grow Alex B. Morrison Library

## 16. Tone and language

Use clear, everyday language.

Main labels should describe what a professor, student, institution, or publisher experiences.

Internal or technical names may appear only in parentheses.

Examples:

- “Files are checked before students can open them” (Malware scanning and quarantine)
- “Each institution sees only its own records” (Tenant isolation and Row Level Security)
- “Large uploads can continue after an interruption” (Resumable TUS upload)
- “Private downloads expire quickly and are recorded” (Signed audited delivery)
- “Books are created from structured Markdown” (Markdown-to-EduBook conversion)
- “Messages that may violate rules can be hidden for review” (Moderation pipeline)
- “Course tools are turned on only when ready” (Feature flags)
- “Public pages appear correctly when shared” (Prerendering and social metadata)

Avoid unexplained terms such as:

- RLS
- Edge Function
- TUS
- SSR
- CRDT
- Webhook
- Tenant
- Schema
- API
- Container worker

When such a term is necessary, explain the user benefit first and place the technical term in parentheses.
